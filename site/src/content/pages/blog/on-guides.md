title: "The project is the journey"

I think one of the most beautiful parts about making projects is the journey it takes you on. All the different places you had to explore, which led to a ton of design decisions you had to make, which then led to even more research.

By the end of it all not only do you have your finished project, but you're also left with an intimate understanding of the problem space, and a story to tell about how you got there; complete with all the blunders and roadblocks.

Step-by-step tutorials, on the other hand, take you straight to the destination. There are no twists, there are no turns, there is no exploration. You are left with a very shallow understanding of the problem space. They rob the person following them of the journey that the project would've otherwise taken them on.

This might seem obvious, but I keep seeing it _again and again_. People writing step-by-step "press this button and move to this coordinate" guides without any explanations.

These things are terrible for teaching the content that they attempt to encapsulate. The only exception is for absolute beginner projects, where the adventure is not actually what you're making, but simply learning the 101's of the medium you're using (usually the software program / pipeline)

The best guides are the ones that give you as little information as possible in terms of upfront info, but instead direct you on where you may want to explore.

---

_Imagine you're on a hike and you need to get to some destination A, with a bunch of checkpoints in-between, and you have 2 options for guidance:_

- _In one of them, at each checkpoint, you are given a sign that points vaguely in the direction of the next checkpoint. You choose the adventure you go on_
- _In the latter, you grab onto a piece of rope that someone drags along. All you must do is hold on_

_Don't let people choose #2._
