_authors note: this is a work-in-progress piece I am still refining_

This is a topic that has been talked about to death but I feel really strongly about sharing my own personal experiences with it here because I think there's a chance it might help someone else. It's the type of thing you know on paper

I recently spent this past year largely working and living remotely. I had very few irl conversations each day, and even online I primarily only talked to 2-3 people a day tops. I had weekly activities (socratica, uoft clubs, etc) but nothing that was consistent during the daytime. Over this period I found it increasingly difficult to do deep, focused work. It seemed as though no matter what I kept getting caught up in a myriad of other activities.

I chalked this up to a matter of discipline. Just need to be more locked in. Just need to have more conviction. After all, in theory, I should've been 10x more productive right? All the essentials to living right next to me. No more 40 minute commutes each way, no more fighting over shared washrooms, no more insane bus trips or Ubers to go places because everything is right next to me, etc. Surely I was clawing out 2-3 valuable hours a day minimum. Imagine all the things I could do during that time!

Turns out, this is how you get stuck in loops and local minima! Without consistently being able to talk to other people, it's easy to get caught in your narrow worldview of existing priors and run in circles about what should be done. Other people go through radically different experiences, which in turn can give radically differing perspectives.

As I started wedging myself in situations where I was consistently talking to people during the day (see: europe), I actually became _more_ focused with the work I was doing.

Social groups provide insurance against pitfalls and traps. Please use them. Being too "locked in" to socialize is a myth.

---

_If this was a framing of productivity, it is generally far more efficient to talk to people who have experiences in whatever you may be interested in rather than trying to re-learn and think through everything through first principles._
