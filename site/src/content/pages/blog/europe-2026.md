Here are the things I learned:

- Life is too short to let things go unsaid. The longer you hold things in the more it eats at your mind. Seek truth at all costs.
- The Open Source Hardware community in Europe is truly fantastic
- You gotta be OK with looking like a dumbass sometimes. I could not speak either Italian or German for the life of me, but nothing bad happened.

Moreover, I think the most interesting thing I learned was how to minimize the amount of noise in my life per day. One of my main goals going into this trip was to learn what it was like to live day-to-day in a place that wasn't North America, which I had done my entire life. I didn't really do any touristy activities per se.

Because I didn't know the language, I ended up trying to minimize the # of human interactions and just general things I needed to do each day so that I could focus on doing my work. What's the minimum # of steps to get from point A to point B? What's the absolute easiest things I could feed myself with?

It's basic stuff like this that really made me re-evaulate what I was doing day to day; I think living in Toronto I could very easily get lost in the sauce with basic questions like "what should I go eat? what transit should I take?" because I understood the language and the friction to exploring these options was very low. It became super easy to get distracted and justify these as "taking advantage of the city" and "work-life balance" that took time away from my ability to do other things. Having the activation energy raised so significantly ended up forcing me to re-evaluate what my priorities really were. I ended up carrying this with me on my way back, which is pretty good!

Anyhow, ended up needing to cut this trip short for a couple of Reasons. I definitely need to visit again at some point. Signing off for now.
